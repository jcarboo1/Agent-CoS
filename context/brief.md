## Parents' talk: core message
The question: whose job is job readiness: the university, the school
or the student?

The evidence Jason brings from the employer side:
- There is a gap between how students rate their readiness and what
  managers see in the first months. The recurring shortfalls are
  pragmatism, working out how to tackle a task without being told,
  and self sufficiency.
- Recent cohorts were affected by lockdown during key teenage years,
  and employers notice it.
- New starters often arrive expecting a different job from the one
  they have (for example, expecting creative work in a role that is
  largely admin) and lack basic workplace tools such as Outlook,
  Excel and PowerPoint.
- Assessment centres test how candidates think and which experiences
  they can speak to, not just grades.

For competition figures, use published research such as the
Institute of Student Employers. Never use figures from Jason's
private meetings.

## What employers actually assess (session content)
- Skills: critical thinking, adaptability, attitude, adaptive
  communication, cooperation, data driven decision making,
  commercial awareness.
- Behaviours that separate candidates: proactivity and initiative;
  owning mistakes and correcting the record; bringing a proposed
  solution with every problem; asking questions; clear, concise
  written communication; emotional maturity.
- Selection trends: more presentations and showcases alongside or
  instead of final interviews; endorsement based on sustained
  performance; structured scoring against a published skills
  framework.

Use these for the consultation and action plan, and later for mock
interviews, a mock assessment centre and a skills checklist.
